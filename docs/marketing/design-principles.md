# MyTrackYo Marketing Design Principles

The reference review reinforced five principles without requiring their visual language: Mixpanel treats its interface as product proof; Tines leads with one concrete proposition and shows workflows; Teak uses a specific voice and varied editorial pacing; Customer.io connects capabilities to use cases; Harvey presents product scenes with restraint and a clear hierarchy. These references were reviewed as principles, not templates.

For MyTrackYo, those principles become:

- **One operator promise:** know arrivals, balances, and room availability. Each section advances one question in the property operator's day.
- **Product before decoration:** five readable scenes replace tiny dashboard illustrations. Every amount and portfolio count comes from one fixed, labelled sample fixture.
- **A varied pace:** warm-paper editorial copy, an ink timeline, unframed narrative sections, a white room board, and a dark portfolio/closing action replace the repeated centered-heading/card pattern.
- **Hospitality-native language:** arrivals, departures, room, folio, advance, and balance replace generic SaaS claims.
- **Proof over borrowed trust:** no customer logos, testimonials, ratings, or unsupported price promises. The trust section states current boundaries plainly.

The brand system keeps the product's pine-green heritage but reserves it for calls to action and key interactions. Brass is a marketing accent only; booking statuses keep their own semantic colours. League Spartan is self-hosted and used throughout the marketing surface.
